## Source-grounded extract (85 words)

For substantial and material noncompliance with a court-approved parenting plan that denies or interferes with parenting time without good cause, the aggrieved parent may file a family access motion stating the specific violations. Upon the required finding, remedies may include compensatory parenting time, counseling, a fine up to $500 payable to the aggrieved party, security for future compliance, and counseling costs. Reasonable expenses, including attorney fees and enforcement costs, may be assessed for good cause against a party who unreasonably denies or interferes with time.

## Structured propositions

- This is an enforcement mechanism, not by itself a substitute for a modification petition.
- The statute directs the court to maintain a simple pro se form and establishes service and disposition timing requirements.
- Specific dates, denied periods, communications, and the absence of good cause are important proof categories.
