## Source-grounded extract (82 words)

Except for the abuse provision, an order concerning decision-making responsibility carries a presumption affecting the burden of proof that joint decision-making is in the best interest of minor children. If the court declines an agreed or requested joint arrangement, it must state its reasons. When abuse under RSA 173-B:1, I has occurred, the court must consider it harmful to children and make allocations that best protect the children or abused spouse; an award of joint decision-making despite abuse requires written findings.

## Structured propositions

- Joint decision-making has a statutory presumption distinct from residential schedule allocation.
- Abuse evidence changes the analysis and triggers protective-order and written-finding requirements.
