## Source-grounded extract (72 words)

In disputed parental-rights or grandparent-visitation cases, including modification requests, the court may order mediation. Unless the court orders otherwise, all relevant issues—including child support, property, and alimony—may also be mediated. The mediator cannot impose a decision, and settlement is voluntary. The court shall not order mediation when there is a domestic-violence finding unless all parties agree. Mediation communications are generally private, privileged, confidential, and inadmissible, subject to RSA 328-C:9.

## Structured propositions

- Mediation may be part of a parenting modification case but does not remove the right to litigate unresolved issues.
- Safety and domestic-violence findings materially affect whether mediation can be ordered.
- The statute provides a funding mechanism when both parties are indigent, subject to Supreme Court rules.
