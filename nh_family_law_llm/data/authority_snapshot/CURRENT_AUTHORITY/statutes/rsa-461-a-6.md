## Source-grounded extract (99 words)

The court must be guided by the child's best interests. Factors include each parent-child relationship and caregiving ability, provision of basic needs and safety, developmental needs, school and community adjustment, support for frequent and continuing contact and for the child's relationship with the other parent, significant relationships, the parents' ability to communicate and cooperate, abuse, incarceration, state policy, and other relevant facts. If the court concludes approximately equal parenting time is not in the child's best interest, it must make supporting findings. No preference may be based on a parent's sex, the child's sex, or a parent's financial resources.
