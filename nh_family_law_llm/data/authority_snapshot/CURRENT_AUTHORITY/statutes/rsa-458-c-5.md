## Source-grounded extract (101 words)

When the parents each bear 50 percent of eligible child-care costs, uninsured medical expenses, and agreed extracurricular expenses, and they have substantially similar incomes and an approximately equal parenting schedule, there is a rebuttable presumption that a $0 child-support obligation is appropriate. With substantially similar incomes and a substantially shared schedule, there is a rebuttable presumption that a deviation is appropriate. When incomes are not substantially similar but the schedule is approximately equal or substantially shared, the guideline calculation may or may not be appropriate. The party relying on this section must prove special circumstances by a preponderance of the evidence.

## Structured propositions

- The statute identifies extraordinary child expenses, significantly high or low income, additional dependents, reasonable parenting expenses, the marital home, tax consequences, parenting schedule, postsecondary expenses, and other special circumstances as possible deviation factors.
- Equal parenting time does not automatically reduce support to zero; the statutory conditions and best-interest analysis still apply.
- For shared/equal schedules without extraordinary circumstances, the order generally should not leave the obligee with higher adjusted monthly income than the obligor after specified taxes and payroll charges.
- The court must make written findings on raised special circumstances.

## Currentness and limits

2024 amendments establishing the current shared/equal-parenting presumptions became effective January 1, 2025.
